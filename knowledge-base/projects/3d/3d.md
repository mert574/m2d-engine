# 3D

Goal: 3D scenes that read as real places, from a library of open materials and models. Roadmap lines: "3D places" and "Asset library".

## Decisions

- Assets and quality follow [[demo-standards]]: free, open assets entering as recipes; the native AAA bar.
- **Model quality is a project setting, not a compile constant.** A project's `quality` block names texture and model levels as caps over each kind's own sizes; the compiler builds every combination and the settings menu swaps them live (docs: Texture and model quality). The lot ships at the low texture level and the middle model level (512 px, 40,000 triangles), the owner's pick from a side-by-side, which brought the published set from 742 MB to 145 MB. Levels are declared lowest first and named, not numbered, so a settings row steps right into more quality. Pages offers no levels (`offerQuality: false` outside the dev server). (16 Nov 2026.)
- **A file size is measured, not guessed.** The 534 MB of the lot's 746 were textures inside the models and 211 geometry, and the trees were half of both; RDO on UASTC saved a fifth of texture bytes for double the encode time at 2k and next to nothing at 1k, so it stays off.

## 3D surfaces

Roadmap line: "3D surfaces". After terrain. Screen-space and planar reflections, water with buoyancy, drag and swimming volumes (simulation, headless; fluid simulation out), decals, simulated GPU particles. **0. Investigation:** open-source libraries for each, performant enough to adopt rather than write.

**Investigation**, 17 Nov 2026: no library is worth adopting; every feature is written here, porting permissive shader code. Licences checked on GitHub; a repository without one is a reference only.
- **Terrain** (first): CDLOD patches on our cull and indirect draws, splat layers in a texture array with height-blended weights. References: Strugar's CDLOD paper; greggman's `webgpu-driving` (plain WGSL clipmaps and CDLOD, no licence: read, don't copy).
- **Water:** FFT ocean kernels from `Noniv/web-ocean` (MIT) or `nff747/ocean-fft-wgsl` (Apache-2.0), Gerstner waves for small water, our own flow maps, and foam, refraction and absorption from the depth we already resolve. Buoyancy is ours: sample the water height at points on the body, apply buoyancy and drag at those points; the simulation evaluates the same waves on the CPU (a few Gerstner waves or a coarse copy), never a GPU readback.
- **Reflections:** screen-space reflections ported from Bevy's `ssr/raymarch.wesl` (MIT or Apache-2.0) or AMD's FidelityFX SSSR (MIT, HLSL), on our normal, depth, velocity and TAA history; planar reflections for water and parallax-corrected box probes written from Lengyel and Lagarde, baked by `environment.js`.
- **Decals:** clustered, as in Doom 2016: decal boxes join the light clusters' compute pass and apply in the mesh shader before lighting. Ports from Bevy's `decal/clustered.wesl`; MJP's `DeferredTexturing` (MIT) as the design reference.
- **GPU particles:** our own: free-list spawning into a ring buffer, a compute step colliding against the previous frame's depth and normal, a radix sort for transparents (ported from `WebGPU-Gaussian-Splat-Viewer`, MIT), drawn through the indirect path. Replaces today's uniform-driven fields.
- Bevy writes its shaders in WESL, which makes the WESL move noted under WebGPU helper libraries cheaper when it comes.

## Backlog

Reviewed against the code on 17 Nov 2026.

**Browsers.** Surveyed 17 Nov 2026: WebGPU is on by default in Chrome and Edge 113+ (Linux only on Intel Gen12+ and NVIDIA under Wayland; Android 121+), Safari 26+ on iOS 26 and macOS Tahoe, and Firefox 141+ on Windows and 147+ on Apple Silicon; about 87% of page views (caniuse). `indirect-first-instance` is exposed everywhere, WebKit included. The docs no longer say "Chromium only". To check on real Safari: the renderer writes every pass's indirect arguments into one buffer, which is the pattern behind a WebKit GPU hang in shipping Safari 26 (bug 321876, fixed upstream Aug 2026; the workaround is one argument copy per draw); Safari 27.2 fixed a render-bundle indirect-draw slowdown; iOS reports `maxBufferSize` between 256 MiB and 1 GiB by device and terminates the page under memory pressure. BC compression and `float32-filterable` are absent on about 40% of Apple adapters, so their fallbacks matter there.

**Places:** terrain with height and splat materials; decals and water; screen-space and planar reflections, and simulated GPU particles (compute over storage buffers, drawn indirect; today's fields are procedural in the vertex stage), all on the main pass's normal, depth and velocity targets — the roadmap's "3D surfaces" item; curved fences, kerbs and roads along paths (`PathStrip`, [[engine-surface]]); scatter that is aware of orientation; a physical atmosphere, clouds and volumetric fog; falling leaves.

**WebGPU helper libraries.** Surveyed 17 Nov 2026 for thin libraries over raw WebGPU (engines excluded). What a library can take is the layout bookkeeping; pipelines, the cull, the post stack and resize lifetimes stay ours, and no engine-agnostic WebGPU frame graph exists outside the engines.
- **Adopt `wgsl_reflect`** (MIT, 1.6.0, ~390k weekly), first in a Node test: reflect every WGSL module and check each struct's offsets and size against the JavaScript constants (`INSTANCE_FLOATS`, `LIGHT_FLOATS`, the uniform offset tables, the bind group layouts). A JavaScript offset drifting from its WGSL struct is the renderer's main silent-bug risk; nothing ships. Generating the offset tables from it can follow.
- **Then, maybe, `webgpu-utils`' structured views** (MIT, 2.1.1) for the low-frequency uniforms (frame, post, environment), raw offsets kept for the per-instance and per-light arrays; only if the test shows layouts are where the bugs come from.
- **WESL** (WGSL with imports and `@if`; MIT, 0.7, pre-1.0) is the likely successor to our `${chunk}` composition: keep chunk names and boundaries module-shaped so the move is mechanical once it reaches 1.0.
- **Not taken:** TypeGPU (pre-1.0 with breaking minors, its value needs TypeScript and a build plugin, and it owns resource creation); `wgpu-matrix` (`mat4.js` is 221 working lines); `wgsl-preprocessor` (template literals already do it); `vgpu` (unproven).

**3D physics parity.** `rapier3dBackend.js` names what the 2D backend has and it does not, each waiting for a game to tune against: a character riding a kinematic platform, and `snapshot`/`restore`, which also gates 3D prediction ([[networking]]).

**Asset library:**

- textures as manifest assets with uids, bound through `uses`, as models and environments already are (`ASSET_TYPES`); a shape's three maps (colour, normal, occlusion-roughness-metal) are what the fetch brings and the renderer reads;
- `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` generated from the fetch scripts' asset lists instead of written by hand;
- a starting set of a few dozen materials and a model set per theme;
- a conifer built for real time: Poly Haven's `pine_tree_01` is 409,000 needle clusters of seventeen triangles each, so any budget under a few hundred thousand keeps one or two in a hundred and the lot's pines read bare. The compiler no longer breaks them (wood keeps a quarter of level 0, foliage thins to whole cards, a quad never shows another card's texels), but the fix for the look is an asset with twig cards.
